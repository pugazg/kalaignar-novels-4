# 16

The letter Colonel Agniyu had written to Colonel Welsh said that the English troops should be led along the routes by the Vaigai, that he would send a contingent from his own force to join them, and that nearly three thousand of Maruthu’s freedom fighters were encamped at several places between Manamadurai and Parthibanur. It also contained war secrets concerning the tactics to be used. It was this letter that had been sent through Urangappuli!

Even without knowing beforehand exactly what the letter contained, Vaalukkuveli had concluded that it must set out some kind of war strategy for defeating the freedom fighters. It was precisely to prevent that letter from reaching Colonel Welsh’s hands that he had spurred Karutha Adappan into action.

Agniyu had thought that if any English soldier travelled alone carrying the letter, the freedom—

<!-- source boundary: scan 138 → scan 139 -->

—fighters would grow suspicious and intercept him. That was why he had entrusted the letter to Urangappuli and asked him to deliver it to Colonel Welsh Durai.

Urangappuli mounted his horse with the letter. After leaving Pattamangalam, he drove the horse hard, repeatedly looking back to see whether anyone was following him. His heart raced with the need to deliver the letter at once to Colonel Welsh Durai, who was camped at a place called Manalur between Madurai and Thiruppuvanam!

Without the slightest concern for people such as the Maruthu Pandiyars, Gopal Nayakkar and Oomaithurai, who were fighting at the edge of death to defend the honour of the land, he swelled with pride at the thought that he was about to perform a sacred duty and urged his horse faster and faster.

Expecting that Urangappuli would have to come from Pattamangalam through Surandaikkadu to reach Manalur, Karutha Adappan turned his horse toward that forest. It was no smooth road! Whenever the horse’s body brushed against the rough thorn-bushes thick among the trees, it hesitated to move forward.

Adappan was bewildered, not knowing which of the many tracks through Surandaikkadu Urangappuli would take. Still seated on his horse, he tilted his head upward and looked at the trees there.

<!-- source boundary: scan 139 → scan 140 -->

A tree towering toward the sky caught his attention. If he climbed up and sat on its highest branch, he could discover which of the many routes Urangappuli’s horse would take. Then he could immediately climb down, follow him, and seize the letter. The idea seemed entirely sound to him.

Leaving the horse hidden among the trees, Adappan climbed onto a branch of the tall tree and sat there. From that height the forest’s branching paths appeared rough, full of rises and hollows. Even when he cast his gaze far into the distance, he could see no one coming on any horse.

Karutha Adappan spent a little while enjoying the sight of deer leaping freely through Surandaikkadu. Then his ears caught the sound of hoofbeats approaching from some distance away. His eyes opened wide.

Yes! Just as he had thought—it was Urangappuli!

Urangappuli’s horse entered Surandaikkadu, paused briefly where three paths met, then chose one of them and began racing along it!

As Adappan scrambled swiftly down from the top of the tree—

<!-- source boundary: scan 140 → scan 141 -->

—his ears caught the hoofbeats of another horse approaching at speed. He looked in astonishment. That horse was coming with a furious pace as if determined to overtake Urangappuli’s! More astonishing still, a woman was riding it!

Who was that woman? Adappan sharpened his memory! Yes—it was Veerammal, whom he had met that day at the Pillaiyar temple in Pattamangalam! Vallatharayan’s sister! Urangappuli’s wife!

Why was she chasing Urangappuli? Adappan was bewildered!

If he remained in the tree merely watching what happened, Urangappuli might escape. So he decided it would be better to follow at once, leapt onto his horse, and turned down the path taken by Urangappuli and Veerammal.

Urangappuli’s horse was faster than before! Veerammal’s horse kept pace behind it! And behind both came Karutha Adappan’s horse, as though its rider were bracing himself for some unexpected turn of events!

“Stop! Stop!”

Veerammal’s cry made the whole forest tremble. Urangappuli looked back at her—

<!-- source boundary: scan 141 → scan 142 -->

—and kicked his horse hard with his heels to make it go even faster! Veerammal, losing patience, drew a sharp dagger from her waist, took aim at Urangappuli’s horse and threw it. The dagger struck the horse squarely in the neck. The horse stopped with a piercing cry and staggered. Urangappuli dismounted before it could fall. Veerammal too stopped her horse and jumped down.

Karutha Adappan, who had followed and watched all this, halted his horse behind a large bush and observed them. Urangappuli’s unattractive face now looked grotesque as well. His burning eyes, like firebrands pulled from a blazing hearth, seemed eager to peck at her. He ground his teeth. The sound was like bamboo trunks rubbing against one another in the forest!

“Veeramma! How much nerve must you have to do a thing like this?”

“Ayyo! Forgive me! I struck down the horse because there was no other way to stop you!”

“You must have aimed at my neck; you missed, and it struck the horse’s neck instead!”

“Do not say such things!”

<!-- source boundary: scan 142 → scan 143 -->

“Then what am I to say? Who are you to interfere in my affairs?”

“I am your wife!”

“Chi! Wife! Shameless woman!”

He struck Veerammal hard across the cheek. She looked at him steadily. “Even if I were not your wife! As a Tamil woman born in this land, I have the right to stop you!” Before she could finish saying it, Urangappuli looped the coarse rope in his hand around her.

“‘The husband is the god one sees with one’s own eyes! Even if he is stone, he is husband; even if he is grass, he is the man! A chaste wife’s beauty lies in obeying her husband!’ Looking at you, I have learned that all this is nothing but ornament for the tongue!”

“The whole world will revile a husband who betrays the land of his birth as a traitor and a vile informer for all time! It is my duty to save my husband from that stain!”

“Oh! You do your duty! I will do mine!”

Dragging Veerammal by the rope, he tied her tightly to a tree. “Veeramma! What will you do now? Stay right here. I will take the horse you came on, deliver the letter to Colonel Welsh Durai, and pick you up on my way back!”

<!-- source boundary: scan 143 → scan 144 -->

She boiled with anger when she heard him. “If you go against my word, when you return you will find only my corpse beneath this tree!”

Urangappuli gave a terrifying laugh. “That is fine too! One nuisance less!”

Hearing his reply, Veerammal burst into tears and cried out.

“Knowing your nature, didn’t they drive you away from the Paganeri house when you went asking for a bride, telling you not even to climb its steps? How many times did I refuse too! How many times did I beg Anna, ‘Choose a bridegroom for me within Pattamangalam nadu itself!’ Yet it seems it was my fate to become the wife of a man who betrays his own people like this!”

Unable to bear Veerammal’s burning words, Urangappuli struck her cheeks again and again with his iron hands.

“Not you—even if God himself came, he could not stop me!” he swore, and moved toward the horse on which she had arrived.

“You do not need God to stop you. Here I am—Karutha Adappan!” Adappan trumpeted as he stepped out in front of him!

“Come then! Come! That day at Colonel Durai’s camp I did not give you what I owed you! Take it now—with interest and principal together!”

<!-- source boundary: scan 144 → scan 145 -->

“Even when your loving wife weeps, laments, sheds tears and cries out, you will not change your mind—will my request change it? Still, it is my duty, so I ask! Give me that letter you are taking to Colonel Welsh!”

Before Adappan had finished speaking, Urangappuli crashed into him! The two rolled and grappled through the black gravel and thorn-bushes on the forest floor! It did not take Adappan long to throw Urangappuli down and seize the letter from his hand. Urangappuli staggered, unable even to rise and walk. Adappan raised his hand to strike him one last time. But hearing Veerammal plead, “Ayyo! Please leave him, Adappa!” Adappan stopped at once. Then he slowly went to her and untied her from the tree.

“For a treacherous informer like this to live in Pattamangalam, right inside the Ambalakkarar’s mansion, is a disgrace to Pattamangalam itself! I am going to tell Vallatharayan immediately!”

“No, thambi, no! Adappa! Forget that anything like this ever happened! At least for the sake of keeping my thaali intact, listen to my plea! I will reform him somehow and preserve Pattamangalam’s honour!”

<!-- source boundary: scan 145 → scan 146 -->

Adappan smiled and accepted her plea. Veerammal supported her husband, who could barely stand and was badly bruised, against her shoulder and took him toward the horse. Adappan helped her mount and helped Urangappuli lean against her. Then Karutha Adappan stood watching as the horse made its way toward Pattamangalam.
