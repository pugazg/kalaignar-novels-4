# 20

Love is what unites two pure hearts! The joining of bodies is a kind of frenzy! Love can be savoured even in old age. The life of lust ends when bodily strength does. Because Vaalukkuveli and Sundarambal had understood this truth, she was not shaken on hearing of his vow. She was ready to wait for him however long it might take.

“Kalyani Nachiyar’s marriage is hardly something impossible! So the time I have to wait will surely be very short,” she thought, gazing eagerly at his face.

“Sundari! Do not misunderstand me! You do not fully know how many problems I am struggling with! If, in the midst of them, I can see my younger sister Nachiyar married, I will have fulfilled the greatest responsibility weighing on my heart.”

<!-- source boundary: scan 174 → scan 175 -->

“I am the pearl in the shell! It is enough for me that I have found a place in the crown that adorns your head!”

“Whatever you may say, Sundari, my ears can hear somewhere in a corner of the palace of your heart the sorrowful undertone of disappointment!”

“If you put a laddu in a child’s hand and, just as the child is about to eat it, take it back saying, ‘Not today, dear; we can eat it another day,’ won’t even the most patient child show some change in its expression?”

“Sundari! I did not come here thinking that an Ambalakkarar was merely visiting the house of a woman artist; I sent the customary seer-varisai! That is all! From today, you too must have the same affection for my younger sister that I have! So, until the thali is tied around Kalyani’s neck, you too must take a vow like mine that there will be no physical union between us!”

“After you have already formed a bond with my body, my heart, my life—with everything in me—the kind of union you speak of is not foremost in our thoughts!”

He gently pressed Sundari’s face against his chest. Her fingers, finer than flower petals, moved over his back! For a little while they stood motionless in that fragrant room.

<!-- source boundary: scan 175 → scan 176 -->

“Sundari!”

“Yes?”

“Here, turn this way!”

She turned. Vaalukkuveli took from his lap a gold chain bearing a tiger-claw pendant. He brought it close to her neck, stopped short of putting it on her, and asked, “Well? You agree, don’t you?”

She clasped his feet. Tears poured from her eyes and bathed his feet. Then she slowly rose.

She bowed her head before the tiger-thali chain in his hand. He placed it around her neck and kissed her forehead. Sundari removed the diamond ring from her own finger and tried to put it on his.

Fortunately, the ring fitted his little finger. The two embraced again! He kissed the thali at Sundari’s neck. She kissed the ring shining on Vaalukkuveli’s little finger.

“Shall I leave, Sundari?” he asked. Before she could answer, the bed itself gave a deep sigh! In the heat of that breath, the jasmine flowers began to wilt. The two descended the stairs and came down.

<!-- source boundary: scan 176 → scan 177 -->

“I have an important task in Madagupatti tonight! I must finish it and be in Paganeri in the morning!” Vaalukku Veli made the demands of his duty clear to her and took his leave.

Sundarambal came as far as the street entrance and stood there watching him depart. After bolting the door, she lay down on the swing in the hall, resting her head on her bent knee.

At first, the swing moved for a while, like the agitation that had seized her moments before; then it came to a stop, as though to show that peace had blossomed within her. Upstairs, the bed, the heap of jasmine, the languor of the incense—everything had lost its arrogance and lay unattended.

Drawing aside the curtain that had covered women writhing in pain, men twisting in the anguish of separation, and lovers playing joyfully in a world of bliss with no place for either—the sun sprouted like a hibiscus in the lower sky. Greeting the rays of dawn, Vaalukku Veli was travelling from Madagupatti toward Paganeri.

In Paganeri, the younger brother Adappan was waiting for his elder brother’s arrival. Following the details in the letter he had sent through Meganathan, he had completed his tasks and reached Paganeri at dawn.

<!-- source boundary: scan 177 → scan 178 -->

Although he had kept the work he had completed hidden from everyone else, Kaadai, Kolari, the servants and the guards in the mansion had already told him—each embellishing the story according to their own imagination—that Vadivambal had come and begun teaching dance to Kalyani Nachiyar, and that Vaalukku Veli had sent seer-varisai to Sundarambal’s house in Tirukkottiyur and had himself gone there that night.

Though he did not like that news, he did not wish to show his feelings openly before his elder brother.

It was early morning, before the town had fully awakened. Even Kalyani Nachiyar was still asleep from the weariness of dance practice. Vaalukku Veli alighted at the entrance of the Paganeri mansion and entered with his own lion-like stride.

With pleasant thoughts that something new—something astonishing—must have happened, the guards, servants, Kaadai and Kolari watched him sharply without taking their eyes off him. They looked at one another and smiled.

“What is it, Kaadai! Kolari!” At the majestic voice of the Ambalakkarar, the two hurried over, bowing and saying, “Ayya!”

“Is Kalyani Nachiyar well?” Affection welled through even that majestic tone.

<!-- source boundary: scan 178 → scan 179 -->

“She is well! She is sleeping!” Kaadai and Kolari answered respectfully and moved aside.

“Adappa! You have come? Nothing dangerous happened, did it?” As his elder brother entered asking this, Adappan rose in the mansion hall.

“Come! Let us go to the room and speak alone!” he said, putting an arm around his younger brother’s shoulder and leading him into the room.

“So even Urangappuli, with feeling for his people and love of country, gave you that secret letter at once!”

“Yes, Anna!” was all Adappan said. He did not want to strip away Urangappuli’s disguise and reveal his true nature, because he was determined to fulfil the promise he had given Veerammal.

“Once Colonels Agniyu and Welsh realized that they could not bend either Vallatharayan or Vairamuthan, somehow they put this Urangappuli into their own pocket! Just as well. That Urangappuli, who was supposed to become a son-in-law of Paganeri, has now gone off to bring disgrace upon the name of Pattamangalam! Leave that aside, Adappa. What did you do with the letter Colonel Agniyu wrote to Welsh Durai? Whom did you deliver it to? Was it of any use?”

<!-- source boundary: scan 179 → scan 180 -->

“Anna! Because I informed the Maruthu forces beforehand that the white army was coming along the Vaigai bank, it became very easy to trap them! I met the commander of Palayakkarar Gopal Nayakkar of Viruppatchi Jallipatti and told him the military secrets the English had set out in Colonel Agniyu’s letter. He immediately sent that information to all the camps!”

“Our men shattered and scattered the white army that came as planned by the forest route along the Vaigai bank, from Manamadurai to Parthibanur! More than ten English military officers were killed! And that is not all: about a hundred of the traitors who betrayed our land, joined them, took up arms and fought against our people were turned into corpses! Your younger brother has the honour of having killed two Englishmen and five traitors!”

“Sabash, Adappa! Sabash! Given our position, this is how we must remain hidden while helping the Maruthu forces and the Nayakkar forces. If we enter this matter openly, we will have to face the terrible opposition of the whites.”

“That is exactly what Gopal Nayakkar’s commander told me too! He said it would be enough if Paganeri, Pattamangalam and the other Kallar nadus simply continued giving the covert help they are giving now!”

<!-- source boundary: scan 180 → scan 181 -->

“What a plight the whites suffered at Parthibanur! Ah! Even Welsh Durai was shaken and retreated with his forces, Anna!”

“Retreating is an art the English have mastered! They call that itself a military strategy! Where have they gone now from Parthibanur?”

“Their next plan is not known! They say a Parangiyar force is heading toward Paramakudi. Colonel Agniyu went to the northern part of Ramanathapuram and, unable to withstand the attacks of our warriors there, was badly shaken and has apparently decided to go to Madurai!”

“Agniyu! He is no ordinary man, Adappa! A great mischief-maker! A mine of intrigue! Cutting apart his nets of deceit is no easy task! What can we do? So many of our own people keep singing the praises of him and his domination!”

“If the victory won by the Maruthu Pandiyars’ warriors at Parthibanur continues, destroying the Parangiyars is certain, Anna—certain!”

“I still have no confidence that final victory will be ours! But that does not mean we are going to stop the support we have been giving to commanders like the Maruthu Pandiyars who are engaged in the liberation struggle, and to palayam rulers like Gopal Nayakkar! Adappa, I have a doubt!”

<!-- source boundary: scan 181 → scan 182 -->

“What is it, Anna?”

“What if Urangappuli gives you that letter pretending to be a good man, then goes to the English colonels and stirs up some trouble against us?... All our secret arrangements would be ruined!”

“I believe he will not do that! Even if that happens, what can we do?...”

“It will not happen! Let it not happen! Let us think only of the good!”

While the elder and younger brothers were speaking, their sister’s voice was heard from outside the room.

“Is the secret over? May I come in?”

“Who is that? Kalyani? Come, amma! Come!” Vaalukkuveli called affectionately.

Kalyani Nachiyar entered carrying an aarathi plate.

“What is the aarathi for, amma? Is it because Adappan’s mission ended in success? How do you know that?” Kalyani looked with a smiling face at Vaalukku Veli as he asked, his words brimming with affection.

“Did Chinna Anna’s mission end in success or failure? I don’t know! This aarathi is because Periya Anna has returned victorious from Tirukkottiyur! All right! Both of you stand there. I’ll take aarathi for both of you together.”

<!-- source boundary: scan 182 → scan 183 -->

“No, Kalyani!” Vaalukkuveli tried to stop her, while Karutha Adappan stood there faltering, unable to say anything... Since she was the cherished younger sister of both men, Kalyani Nachiyar circled the aarathi before them, her heart and face like a red lotus blooming that very moment!

Seeing Meganathan arrive then, Vaalukkuveli asked, “Any new news?”

The eagerness in that question came from wondering whether the decline of the white army was continuing.

“They say the English colonels have concluded that the crushing defeat the whites suffered at Parthibanur was caused by the help given to the Maruthu Pandiyars and Gopal Nayakkar by many of our Kallar nadus—especially our Vattakottai Nadu—and, above all, by the heroic deeds of our Adappan. However secretly we aid the liberation forces, the white dogs manage to sniff it out correctly.”

Hearing Meganathan’s explanation, Vaalukkuveli showed no agitation and asked, “So what do you say we should do now?”

“Gopal Nayakkar has called an emergency meeting of the Ambalakkarars of our Kallar nadus today in the Mavali Kanmai area near Kottadiyur. A message has come saying that you, Adappan—

<!-- source boundary: scan 183 → scan 184 -->

—and I must attend without fail.”

“Is that so? We must certainly attend. But no one must know that we are going there. Kalyani! Do not let even a word of it fall into anyone’s ear! Adappa! Make the necessary arrangements. I too will be ready in a moment!”

Even as they were speaking, a carriage came and stopped outside the entrance. Vadivambal, Lalithangi and Nathamuni got down from it. As Vaalukkuveli, Adappan, Meganathan and Kalyani were walking and talking near the entrance, Vadivambal’s group greeted all four respectfully.

Vadivambal turned her eyes into fishhooks and cast them at Vaalukkuveli. Though he noticed, Vaalukkuveli acted as though he had not, and looking at his sister said with a smile, “What is this, amma! Looks as though the dance teacher has arrived! Hmm, let it go on! We will go and come back soon.”

Letting her upper garment slip slightly—as a deliberate ploy to draw his eyes like iron to a magnet the instant he saw it—Vadivambal asked Vaalukku Veli, “Why? You could stay and see whether the dance training is going well.”

“Do I need to give yet another testimonial to Sundari’s younger sister? Let the practice go on! Another—

<!-- source boundary: scan 184 → scan 185 -->

—day I will watch it. We are setting out on a very important matter.”

Not only did that answer fail to satisfy Vadivu; she could not bear the way Vaalukku Veli, by mentioning Sundari, made it seem that her only distinction was being Sundari’s younger sister. Without showing the resolve she had formed to ensnare Vaalukkuveli somehow, she laughed and talked with Kalyani Nachiyar and entered the mansion with her group.

For a little while Vaalukkuveli stood staring only at the word “Sundari” written on the curtain hanging from the Kottadiyur carriage parked at the entrance. What did he care about Vadivambal, who had stepped down from that carriage?

Was it not the letters spelling ‘Sundari’ on the curtain that had left him standing there completely entranced?

“Shall we move?” Hearing Meganathan’s voice, Vaalukkuveli moved away from the entrance with an awkward smile. By then the saarat carriage arranged on Adappan’s orders had arrived. Vaalukku Veli and Meganathan climbed into it, and Adappan set the carriage moving.

Inside, Kalyani Nachiyar prepared for dance practice and stood before Vadivambal. Nathamuni beat the tala. Lalithangi began to sing. Kalyani danced. Vadivu gave corrections from time to time.

<!-- source boundary: scan 185 → scan 186 -->

Just then, cooks Kaadai and Kolari came running in, shouting! Everyone started in alarm.

“Amma! Amma! They say the Pattamangalam jallikattu bull has been let loose. It has gored and torn several people along the way and is running toward our Paganeri road—if it enters Paganeri, that will be the end of it!!”

Hearing this, Nathamuni trembled all over, clutched Lalithangi and cried, “Ayyo! Someone call a man!”

“Oh God! Ambalakkarar Ayya isn’t here! Chinnayya isn’t here! There is no one at all! What outrage is this? Where is this going to end?”

Kaadai and Kolari wailed.

“Stay calm! I will go myself! Kaadai! Tell them to harness my box carriage at once! Kolari! You come with me!”

Vadivu and the others broke into a sweat at Kalyani’s fearlessness. Kalyani picked up a dagger lying there. She removed her dance clothes, changed into other clothes, tucked the dagger into her waist, and walked quickly toward the box carriage being readied at the entrance.
