“It wasn't that we did not want to come to the Tirukkottiyur chariot festival! At that very time I had to face a wrestler in Madurai. My elder brother came with me to watch that contest! He sent you a letter even then, explaining why we could not come.”

“Wonderful Madurai! Wonderful Nayak king! One day for the wrestling! Another day to receive the award! And so, whenever there is a palace function, you keep running about like waves?”

“It would be better if you and my elder brother discussed all this between yourselves. I am a young man. If I say something rash, the hostility that is somehow already smouldering may begin to spit fire.”

“Why all this useless talk? Tell them plainly that the man of Pattamangalam will not come to the Paganeri chariot festival.”

Vairamuthan, who had remained patient until then, tried to calm his elder brother Vallatharayan.

“Anna! There is nothing you do not know. Should we magnify mistakes that have already happened and make room for many more mistakes to occur? Whether it is Vaalukku Veli or Karutha Adappan, they have accepted our invitations and come to so many functions held in Pattamangalam. It was not that they did not want to come to the chariot festival we held at Tirukkottiyur this year; they were simply unable to come! That's all! Even if

<!-- source boundary: scan 27 → scan 28 -->

we assume they deliberately stayed away, by going now to the Paganeri chariot festival, can't we show the townspeople our broad-mindedness and their narrow-mindedness? The Ambalakkarars who lead Paganeri and Pattamangalam may be different men, but isn't the Lord common to both nadus? Let us go to Paganeri too and worship that Lord!”

Vallatharayan stood motionless, listening closely to his younger brother's counsel.

“There is some justice in what Vairamuthan says! Paganeri and Pattamangalam are not even ten kal apart! Why should there be so much enmity between them? I too have been working tirelessly, placing the burden on Madurai Meenakshi, because somehow I want to bring the two of you together. So it would be best to heed your brother and go to the festival without rejecting Vaalukku Veli's invitation!”

These words of Vellai Ayyar too threw Vallatharayan into confusion. Veerammal wanted to say something on her part:

“Listen to what your younger brother says and what Ayyar says, and just this once grant forgiveness to the Paganeri people, Anna!” she said, in a tone brimming with hauteur.

<!-- source boundary: scan 28 → scan 29 -->

Karutha Adappan's moustache quivered. “Forgiveness!” The word struck his head as though with a sledgehammer! He would have flared up! He would have spread there like a forest fire of fury! But the advice his elder brother Vaalukku Veli often gave him about valour and composure rushed into his mind and collided with his anger.

What if his elder brother were hurt and said, “You behaved uncivilly at the very place you went to deliver an invitation, Thambi”? A weary sigh at that thought held back his rage.

Fortunately, by then Vallatharayan had agreed to attend the opening of the Paganeri chariot festival.

Adappan's lips, which had been itching to whirl words like a storm, became flowers of a smile. He shook Vairamuthan's hand and expressed his appreciation.

Karutha Adappan, who had been troubled that the seeds of an undesirable catastrophe might be sown, possessed the firmness of mind to face whatever came; yet because he was careful that his own manner of approach should not cause a mistake and bring blame upon him, he praised Vairamuthan's measured conduct for paving the way to an amicable outcome.

Vallatharayan looked at his younger brother Vairamuthan and smiled softly. Vairamuthan too, looking sideways at his elder brother, gave a faint smile.

<!-- source boundary: scan 29 → scan 30 -->

Once again asking them to arrive at the festival at the appointed time, Karutha Adappan mounted his horse and set off.

As the horse turned along the road to Paganeri, circling the Pattamangalam Pillaiyar temple, a spear was raised and held ready to fly straight at Adappan's chest from a crumbling niche in the temple's compound wall!

The moment the horse came alongside the wall, the hand gripping the spear began to hurl it—but before it could do so, another warrior's hand stopped that arm!
