Without waiting for Sundarambal’s reply, Karutha Adappan, who had already reached the doorway, turned back and said, “Bolt the door! Be careful! If you need any help, send word to Paganeri at once!” After warning her, he mounted his horse, which stood ready in the street, and rode away.

Cursing Colonel Durai’s men, Sundarambal let out a deep sigh and sat on the swing. The maid came near her and said, trembling, “Amma! For Ambalakkarar Ayya’s younger brother, that Adappan, to have had to come here—things must have become terribly dangerous!”

“Yes, girl! But I have another fear! What sort of trouble might come from that Adappan! Good thing I escaped without the headache going away only for a screw-pain to take its place.”

“No, Amma—he seems like a good young man, Amma!”

“No matter how good a young man he may be, in front of women men are like wax kept beside a fire! The word ‘men’ itself means ‘weakness’!”

Having delivered this concluding pronouncement, Sundari hurried to the worship alcove there and stood praying before the Nataraja statue.
