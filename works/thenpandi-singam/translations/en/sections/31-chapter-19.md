# 19

Realizing her own faltering, Vadivambal reproached herself. Thinking that her desires had turned into disappointments, just like the milk spilling from the pot rolling across the floor, she stood staring at the flowing milk and the scattered fruit.

Her mind mocked her! “What right do you have, girl, to roll about with your dream lover in the bedchamber of your elder sister’s house? Is Sundari to blame because you were deceived and handed him over to her?”

The mind that asked this immediately changed sides and gave an anger-soaked answer of its own! “Even if she is your elder sister, was it right for her to entrust the things needed for the bedchamber to you without thinking that Vadivambal too is a grown woman? Look, girl, look! Isn’t this Sundari’s challenge: ‘I have won’?”

<!-- source boundary: scan 164 → scan 165 -->

While the young woman stood beside the bed amid this battle within her mind, Lalithangi came rushing in and was shocked by the spilled milk and the tray in disarray. Of course she understood Vadivambal’s condition!

Putting the fruits back on the tray and wiping the milk from the floor with a cloth, Lalithangi began to hold forth: “Amma Vadivu! No one can change fate! With beauty like yours, ten thousand men like the Paganeri Ambalakkarar would lie in penance on the steps of your house! You need only cast a glance—there are any number of palayakkarars standing there grinning, ready to pour all the paddy in their granaries at your feet!”

Vadivambal felt that she might find a little relief if she could pour out the anguish within her to someone. Lalithangi was just the person for that.

“Say whatever you like! A jealousy I never knew was in me has arisen in my heart toward my elder sister! She is my sister, isn’t she? Shouldn’t the younger sister be satisfied when she is happy? Don’t ask me that! If it were a tasty appam—and a hot appam at that—Vadivambal could simply let Sundarambal eat it! But what my sister has taken from me is neither an appam nor an adhirasam! When she has taken away my very life, how can I possibly bear it?”

<!-- source boundary: scan 165 → scan 166 -->

“Oh, what is this talk of life, Vadivu! Isn’t life something we shape by looking after ourselves? What would you do if Vaalukkuveli Thevar had never been born and only Vadivambal had been? If there were no Vaalukkuveli, some other fence for a field would have come along!”

“Let any number of fences come! I will allow them all to occupy my bed! But the one I need to occupy my heart is Vaalukkuveli! I will give my body to those who heap up gold and jewels like the sea, but I will keep waiting for Vaalukkuveli to satisfy the desire in my heart!”

“Then shall I do one thing? Shall I go to your elder sister right now and make her hand him over to you?”

“Is love alms? And would you put in a recommendation for me? Lalithangi-amma! Just watch whether I defeat the elder sister who has ruined my desire with my own charms and daring!”

“Charms! Daring! With whom? Your elder sister? What kind of flirtation can there be between one woman and another, amma?”

“I am burning in pain! Don’t make fun of me! If I do not turn the Paganeri Ambalakkarar into an ascetic doing penance in my bedchamber, my name is not Vadivambal!”

Lalithangi stood stunned! She realized

<!-- source boundary: scan 166 → scan 167 -->

that there was no point in speaking to Vadivu any further. “All right, amma Vadivu! May victory be yours!” she blessed her.

Just then Nathamuni’s voice came from below: “Lalithangi! Come, come! Ayya has arrived!”

Vadivu stood there suppressing her seething anger. Lalithangi ran down the stairs. Vadivambal too slowly walked down those steps as though showing how a lifeless body would walk.

In the reception hall, Sundarambal stood with a beautiful rose garland. She placed it around the neck of Vaalukku Veli as he entered, touched his feet and bowed. After returning Nathamuni and Lalithangi’s greetings, Vaalukku Veli sat on the swing there.

Sundari, who had been gazing at him without even blinking, suddenly remembered and called, “Vadivu! Vadivu!” so that she could introduce her.

“Vadivu has gone to her own house!” Lalithangi replied.

“Don’t the two of you live in the same house?” Vaalukkuveli asked.

“They dance on the same stage! They live in two houses!” Nathamuni said with a foolish grin. By then Lalithangi had brought the betel-leaf tray and placed it on the swing.

<!-- source boundary: scan 167 → scan 168 -->

“How interested is Kalyani Nachiyar in the art of dance? Didn’t Vadivambal say anything?”

Lalithangi and Nathamuni competed with one another to answer the question.

“She dances magnificently right from the start!”

“Because her foundation is strong, she picks up whatever she is taught immediately!”

Delighted by these assessments, Vaalukkuveli took some areca nut from the betel tray and began chewing it, then reached to smear lime on a betel leaf. Sundari came near at once, took the leaf from his hand, smeared the lime herself, folded it and gave it to him.

The beauty with which she folded and offered it, and the pleasure with which he took and savoured it, made Nathamuni and Lalithangi speak to each other with sidelong glances. The two slowly moved away from there.

Pretending not to notice them slipping away, Sundari kept folding betel leaves for Vaalukkuveli. She did not remember how many leaves she folded. Neither did he. But how much lime she had smeared on without measure became clear only when his entire mouth began to burn! He managed to keep from showing that his mouth was burning.

Realizing her mistake, Sundari immediately ran off, brought the betel spittoon and

<!-- source boundary: scan 168 → scan 169 -->

held it out. Wasn’t Vaalukku Veli a warrior? Could he let even the slightest sign of embarrassment over his burning mouth show? He gestured for her to put the spittoon down. But she refused and kept holding it in her hand near his mouth. With no other choice, he spat the taamboolam into it. His tongue, lips and gums were all burning.

“Forgive me! I put on too much lime, didn’t I?” she asked in words as sweet as mango juice.

“Just as tala and laya must have the right measure in dance, shouldn’t the areca nut, lime and betel leaf have the right measure in taamboolam too?”

“I’ve never had the habit of folding it like this before!”

“From now on, I’ll teach you!”

“If you drink a little milk, it will soothe your mouth.”

“Milk? You mean—which paal among the three paals?”

“None of the three! Cow’s milk!”

“If you have some, give it to me!”

“Let’s go to where it is!”

“To where the cow is? You mean the cowshed?”

<!-- source boundary: scan 169 → scan 170 -->

“You are not merely a king of music; you are a king of humour too!”

“Not bad! Along with the art of Bharata, you are skilled in the art of conferring titles!”

“That too suits only those who deserve them! All right, come!... Let’s go upstairs!”

His ears drank the nectar of affection that flowed through her invitation! Yet he felt strangely shy. He had won garlands of victory in countless sword-battles and walked with his chest held high. He had entered forests to hunt and brought fierce tigers and leopards down at his feet. Yet now, without raising his head to look at Sundari, he turned it this way and that as he spoke. In the animal world the tiger conquers the deer! In the human world the deer conquers the tiger—his own case had proved that truth with striking clarity!

Sundarambal slowly went to the street door and bolted the front doors. Turning toward the kitchen, she asked, “Is the food ready?”

Lalithangi ran over, stuck only her head out from behind a curtain and said, “Everything is ready! Food, milk, fruit—everything is waiting upstairs, amma!” Sundari again looked at him and said, “Hmm! Come!”

He remained seated on the swing without getting up. Sundari stretched out her hands, took his hands in hers and said, “Come.”

<!-- source boundary: scan 170 → scan 171 -->

What was left to do? The deer had conquered the tiger! The two climbed the stairs and went to the upstairs room. Food was waiting for them! The soft bed, which had been starving for food for a long time, was waiting too! Jasmine has its own splendour of fragrance. And if the whole bed was jasmine, what more was there to ask! In the golden light of the lamp, her golden body gleamed still more brightly as she began serving him food on a silver plate.

“You must sit too!” Vaalukkuveli said, taking her by the shoulder and seating her. Those slender shoulders of hers, which had swayed and swayed in dance all these years, felt his hand and sent through her whole body a sensation of pleasure she had never known before. Sitting close beside one another, the two ate.

“Today is the first day! So there is no meat!” she said.

“What if there is no venison or rabbit meat? Can they equal the taste of these mangoes?” he asked, looking at her face. Was it only her face he looked at? A smile showing that she understood flashed and vanished as lines of shyness across her pomegranate-red cheeks. An unfamiliar warmth spread through Sundari’s body.

At last the meal came to an end. He held out his hand over the silver plate and she washed it herself. She even took a cloth and dried his wet hand. Within a few moments, the place where they had eaten was cleaned.

<!-- source boundary: scan 171 → scan 172 -->

Vaalukku Veli rose and sat on a chair near the bed. Sundari sat on the floor opposite him, peeling fruit segments and handing them to him one by one.

After putting a few segments in his hand, she pouted and insisted that she would now feed them only into his mouth. At last he agreed. She fed him one or two pieces, then brought another to his lips; when he opened his mouth, she put it into her own and laughed, “Kuluk!”

He took the fruit segment from her hand and put it into her mouth. She gently bit his fingers.

Pulling his finger away, Vaalukkuveli looked at her and asked, “Yes! If you have so much affection for me, why did you speak to me with such hatred that day?” His fingers were combing through her hair.

“Doesn’t a cool waterfall flow through the midst of black rock? It is just like that—this waterfall of affection has arisen amid our harsh quarrel!”

Hearing Sundari’s answer, he sat there, forgetting himself. She too buried her head in his thigh and floated in pleasure.

The soft bed writhed with hunger! Eager at the thought that by satisfying its hunger they were about to give it life—

<!-- source boundary: scan 172 → scan 173 -->

—for the very first time, Sundari slowly rose and put out the lamps that were burning brightly in the room. Only one small lamp remained, glowing dimly.

“Aren’t you sleepy? You must be very tired! Why don’t we lie down?” she asked in a trembling voice. By then she had swallowed her saliva twice.

“No, Sundari! I am leaving for Paganeri!” he said.

She became a yaazh whose string had snapped. “Why? Why?” she asked, embracing him and drawing him toward the bed. Her eyes were clouded with tears.

“Sundari! I desire you! You alone are my companion in life! But... but...”

“But what?...”

“A vow I have undertaken prevents us from easing the anguish of our separation!”

“A vow?”

“Yes! Until my younger sister Kalyani’s marriage is completed...”
