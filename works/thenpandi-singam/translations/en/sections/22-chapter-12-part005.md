It is my responsibility to warn him not to clash with a mountain and bring Paganeri around to your way! My view is that you should not rush into hostility with Paganeri.”

Agniyu poured another cup of liquor into his stomach. An arrogant laugh rose in him at the thought that all the revolutionaries standing against the English advance could be scattered like bits of straw in a gale. Staggering over to Vellai Ayyar, he shook him by the shoulder and crowed loudly:

“Remember the date—16 October 1799! That was the day Veerapandiya Kattabomman was hanged from a tamarind tree at Kayatharu! Oomaithurai, who had been held in the Palayamkottai prison, escaped and, from Panchalankurichi once again, fought from the ninth of February to the twenty-fourth of May in this year 1801, lost the fort, and fled to the Maruthu Pandiyars! Within another six months, this Colonel Agniyu’s army will certainly surround him, the Maruthu brothers who support him—and whoever else they may be! Does Vaalukkuveli want to be caught in that storm and die? Or does he want to live with our good wishes?”

Vellai Ayyar understood that Colonel Durai was drunk and spoke calmly.

“When Vaalukkuveli is not your enemy, why do you yourself want to go out of your way and turn him into one?”

<!-- source boundary: scan 106 → scan 107 -->

“Mr. Vellai! If you bring Vaalukkuveli around, I will be delighted! I am ready to welcome you and praise you for it! If he does not fall into line, there is always the British Empire’s divide-and-rule stratagem! Once they are caught in that stratagem, we can finish building the graves of both your beloved Ambalakkarars without a single bullet from the English army being fired! Both are powerful Ambalakkarars. It is only because I ask myself why I should seek their hostility too that I have tolerated their refusal to accept one of my plans! At the same time, it is not something that can be tolerated if both of them, or even one of them, consort with our enemies! Even so, I have been tolerating it as though I know nothing.”

“Agniyu, leave that matter to me! I am on close terms with both Vaalukku Veli and Vallatharayan! As far as I know, they have done nothing against you! If, without even my knowing it, secret plots of the kind you describe are indeed taking place, it is my duty to keep them under control and make them loyal to English rule! They are good friends of mine! Can I simply stand by and watch them and their nadu be ruined?”

“May your effort succeed as far as possible! Otherwise, I can set the two goats butting each other and drink the blood that flows!”

<!-- source boundary: scan 107 → scan 108 -->

“Do not drink that blood! Drink what you already have in your hand!”

Colonel Agniyu emptied the cup and flung it down. The army’s deputy commander hurried into the camp tent...

Looking at him, Agniyu asked, “Urgent news? Why is there so much agitation on your face?”

The deputy commander gave the Colonel a military salute and delivered the news in alarm.

“One of our forces left Thiruppuvanam for Thiruppachethi to oppose the Maruthu Pandiyars’ forces. Within that eight-mile distance, the damage Maruthu’s warriors inflicted on our force was immense! The formidable ‘Major Grey’ has been killed. The commanders Blacker, Cole, Farmington and Stuart have suffered severe wounds. Many soldiers have lost their lives. The Maruthu troops hacked to pieces the body of Major Grey, who was shot and killed in the battle!”

Hearing the deputy commander’s report, Colonel Agniyu was shocked! He flew into a rage! The stupor brought on by drinking himself full cleared away!

He immediately ordered his forces to set out for Thiruppachethi. Major—

<!-- source boundary: scan 108 → scan 109 -->

—Grey had been hacked to pieces; Agniyu too prepared at once to leave with that force, determined to hack the Maruthu Pandiyars to pieces and make a feast of them for the vultures.

“Mr. Vellai! I am leaving now,” he said, seething with anger as he shook Ayyar’s hand.

“I pray that, by God’s grace, no danger should come to your life,” Vellai Ayyar said, offering his blessing. He felt a sense of relief. Comforted that this new and alarming news had turned Colonel Agniyu’s anger at the Paganeri and Pattamangalam men in another direction, Ayyar left the place.

A conch began to sound, calling the camp soldiers to make ready at once for the journey! Hearing that conch blast, his body trembling and his heart in turmoil, Vellai Ayyar moved slowly away in fear, looking back at the camp!
