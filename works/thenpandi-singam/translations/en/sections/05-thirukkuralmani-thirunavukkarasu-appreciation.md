# Thirukkuralmani Thirunavukkarasu — Appreciation!

In a way that honours the self-respect that has been the very breath of the Tamil people—in a way that makes clear that a Tamil who has lost self-respect will not live—Kalaignar has created *Thenpandi Singam* for us.

I looked into whether there were any other historical references concerning Vaalukku Veli, the hero of this story. The history of Tamil Nadu itself has not yet been written fully. Fourteen years ago, Kalaignar formed a large committee and guided it to write the history of Tamil Nadu systematically. When that work is not now proceeding properly—when even the history of ancient times has not yet been written—where are we to find the histories of more recent times?

In this novel, the man who seeks to eliminate Vaalukku Veli is Urangaappuli. An English reference identifies him as Oyyappuli Thevan. He is a coward.

That the characters Kalaignar has created seem like people living among us is a particular strength of the creator. The way he has linked all the events of the story, given them a form, and shaped them into a coherent structure makes this novel not a heap of bricks, but a well-built, attractive mansion.

<!-- source boundary: scan 9 → scan 10 -->

Kalaignar has another art at his command. In the use of words, in giving explanations to words, he is an accomplished expert. For example: “Brother, what did you think? Did you think Pattamangalam was a mangalam whose honour had withered away? Pattamangalam is the mangalam that won a title for valour.”

The life-breath of this novel is the self-respect of the Tamil; its aim is that a united Tamil land should come into being; the time when Tamil may receive the Nobel Prize is drawing near.
