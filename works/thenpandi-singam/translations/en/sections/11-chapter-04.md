# 4

Knowing nothing either of the attempt that had been made to kill him with a spear or of the fact that the attempt had been stopped, Adappan urged his horse along the road to Paganeri, hurrying to tell his elder brother the good news.

“Urangaappuli! What were you trying to do! It is an act utterly unworthy of the honour of our clan and of the way of warriors! If you possess manliness and strength, you should dare to face Adappan directly! To hide and kill a warrior like a coward is a disgrace to our very people!”

Vairamuthan hurled the words at his brother-in-law Urangaappuli!

The news that Urangaappuli had tried to kill Adappan by hiding behind the compound wall and throwing a spear at him reached Vallatharayan too. He did not approve. Even Veerammal rebuked her husband for what he had done.

<!-- source boundary: scan 31 → scan 32 -->

Urangaappuli, who had eagerly imagined that he was about to accomplish some great deed and receive praise for it from the Pattamangalam Ambalakkarar, received only disappointment and ridicule! But they kept the incident within the family and did not allow it to spread outside.

Vairamuthan pointed out that taking the rough and hot-headed Urangaappuli, who bore such anger and hatred toward Adappan, to the Paganeri chariot procession might cause some unwanted calamity. So Vallatharayan told Urangaappuli and Veerammal to remain in Pattamangalam and set out for Paganeri with Vairamuthan.

The Pattamangalam brothers began their journey in a handsome box-cart decorated with brass plates and bronze bands, drawn by two well-fed bulls. In front of and behind the cart, armed warriors ran in large numbers, as though competing with one another in carrying out their guard duty.

The Paganeri chariot stood decked out like a Bharatanatyam dancer. The toy horses fixed to its front seemed to be galloping on all four legs, as though they themselves were about to pull the chariot swiftly away.

<!-- source boundary: scan 32 → scan 33 -->

The chariot would move only when hundreds and thousands of labourers and townspeople grasped the ropes and pulled! And was that all? Some had to lever the chariot wheels from behind with heavy poles!

Others had to work with skill and judgment, placing chocks here and there so that the wheels would turn along the proper path, the chariot would move correctly through the street, and finally return to its station.

But children, unaware of all these difficulties, imagine that the toy horses fixed to the front are the ones pulling the chariot. Is it only with a chariot? In the country, when so many labourers work until sinews are strained and bones are broken so that countless good things may happen, how many “toy horses” put on a false show, declaring that everything happened because of them! And are not the simple people who believe them even more childlike than children themselves!

People from Paganeri, Katteri, Nagarampatti and the other villages of Paganeri Nadu gathered in great crowds to watch the chariot procession and to delight in the arrival of the Ambalakkarars of many Kallar nadus.

The flood-like crowds surged through the chariot streets, hearts gladdened as they told one another that Vaalukkuveli would first receive the silk parivattam honour at the Paganeri Sivan temple and would then inaugurate the chariot procession.

<!-- source boundary: scan 33 → scan 34 -->

Most of the invited Ambalakkarars had arrived at the Sivan temple mandapam; Adappan welcomed and attended to them all, seating them on the chairs arranged in the hall.

Before long, the Pattamangalam Ambalakkarar's box-cart stopped at the entrance of the Sivan temple. Adappan ran forward, welcomed Vallatharayan and Vairamuthan with a pleasant face, and led them into the mandapam.

Vallatharayan grumbled to Vairamuthan, with rising irritation, that Vaalukkuveli had still not arrived even after making them wait. While Vairamuthan was calming his elder brother, the Sivan temple's nagara, ekkalam, vanga and the blasts of various trisarna instruments shook the sky. Those instruments were sounding to announce Vaalukku Veli's arrival! At the temple entrance, large numbers of people stood in two rows awaiting their leader.

The Paganeri Ambalakkarar's carriage stopped a short distance away. The moment Vaalukkuveli stepped down, the crowd roared, “Long live the Ambalakkarar!” Men and women alike leapt and danced, crying, “Long live Vaalukkuveli Thevar!” Bowing to them with great humility—yet without the slightest loss of his stately gait—that lion called Vaalukku Veli walked toward the temple.

<!-- source boundary: scan 34 → scan 35 -->

A young woman too alighted from the carriage and followed Vaalukku Veli. One could say she was a little bird at that age just reaching eighteen.

One could know it without touching her: a body like silk! A row of teeth that seemed to make pearls and jasmine jealous! Looking at those teeth, one might imagine that nature had powdered the milk-white moon and set those tiny particles of light into her coral-red lips!

The old habit of describing eyes as arrows, spears or fish was a thing of the past! Those who saw her eyes could only stand longing, unable to find a comparison in any language! A face rippling with light! A body so formed that it seemed to have been sculpted with life itself!

Poets would feel compelled to write that perhaps the beautifully decorated chariot standing there had itself taken the form of a woman and was now walking behind Vaalukku Veli!

“Kalyani Nachiyar! Kalyani Nachiyar!” Soft voices rose here and there in the crowd, overflowing with affection.

Entering the temple mandapam, Vaalukku Veli welcomed all the visiting Ambalakkarars with folded hands. “Ambalakkarar of Melanadu! Come! Greetings. Nadunadu people,

<!-- source boundary: scan 35 → scan 36 -->

come! Very happy to see you! Oh! Are you from Sirukudi Nadu? Are you well? Ambalakkarar of Vellur Nadu, come!... Ah! Pattamangalam people! Come! Vairamutha, come, come!...” As he mingled warmly with the Ambalakkarars, he said to the beautiful young woman who had followed him, “Little sister! Offer your greetings to all of them,” and then introduced her to the Ambalakkarars: “This is my only sister—my beloved sister, Kalyani Nachiyar.”

Kalyani Nachiyar, her youthful beauty swaying, fell at the feet of the Ambalakkarars and paid her respects. She did not hesitate even to fall at Vairamuthan's feet. Only when she rose did her eyes hesitate, unable at once to leave his face.

Worship and deeparadhana were performed for Sivan in the temple, the bells rang out, and the temple bestowed the silk parivattam honours upon Vaalukku Veli.

“Thank you very much, all of you, for honouring my request and coming! Come, let us begin the chariot procession by taking hold of the rope. No one should set out on the road thinking the festival is over once the chariot procession ends! I have arranged places for all the Ambalakkarars to stay. Please also honour my home by coming for a feast. First let us go to the chariot stand. Come!”

<!-- source boundary: scan 36 → scan 37 -->

Vaalukku Veli, Kalyani Nachiyar and Karutha Adappan went ahead, while the Ambalakkarars followed them.

For many, the joy of seeing all the Ambalakkarars together was greater than the joy of seeing the chariot festival itself.

When the Ambalakkarars came to touch and pull the rope tied to the front of the chariot and formally begin the procession, Vellai Ayyar was already waiting there ahead of them. “Come! Come! The sky is going to pour rain! The rain-dependent country is going to prosper! What is this—all the Ambalakkarars have come together in unity!” he kept saying.

Ayyar's sharp eyes did not fail to note the change in Vallatharayan's expression and the new radiance that had appeared on Vairamuthan's face! Leaning toward Vallatharayan, he said softly into his ear, “Doesn't this Vaalukku Veli behave as though he were the leader of all the Ambalakkarars!”

Vallatharayan nodded in agreement and ground his teeth. “Why, the proper time has come! Shall we begin? Shall we take hold of the rope?” As Vellai Ayyar called out in this way, Vaalukku Veli folded his hands, worshipped the deity on the chariot, and touched the rope.

All the Ambalakkarars came forward to touch the rope and pull. Vairamuthan took one side of the rope,

<!-- source boundary: scan 37 → scan 38 -->

while Kalyani Nachiyar began pulling from one side of the parallel rope.

Explosive festival charges boomed. The levering poles went to work. Thousands pulled the chariot. As everyone pulled at speed, a cross-block placed before the chariot brought it to a jolting halt. Those holding the rope fell over one another and got back up. Kalyani Nachiyar was one of those who fell! On whom did she fall? On Vairamuthan himself! She rose, her face turning red with shyness like a ripe kovai fruit.
