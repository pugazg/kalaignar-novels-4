# 8

Somehow steadying her heart, Kalyani Nachiyar lifts a silver plate filled with dishes and comes near the dining table. The eyes of everyone seated before the leaves swarm and circle around her. But her small blue eyes search for him and find him. He too sits there admiring the grace with which she walks.

It seems to her as though this Ambikapathi is singing while looking at her, “Each step she sets down aches; each step she lifts blisters.” The custom is to serve the person seated first at the dining table and then serve each person in turn. But she no longer remembers that her own brother is there, and she forgets that the Pattamangalam Ambalakkarar is the principal guest.

She goes straight to Vairamuthan’s leaf to serve the dishes. The wings of all those swarming eye-bees spread wide in astonishment and begin to flutter. The moment she reaches Vairamuthan’s leaf, her head bows of its own accord. For a short

<!-- source boundary: scan 62 → scan 63 -->

while there is silence, complete stillness. Then she takes a dish and places it on his leaf. She keeps placing more and more. He stretches out a hand in a gesture that says enough. But she has forgotten herself; she has forgotten everyone around her too!

Vairamuthan’s leaf rises like a hill of rabbit meat and venison! Eggs roll about separately! Realizing that gestures are useless, Vairamuthan says, “Enough! Enough!” with an awkward smile.

Those words must have sounded like sweet music to her—when the two words rang out clearly, Kalyani lost her composure. Yet her hands did not stop. Everything that had been on the silver plate moved onto his leaf.

With no other choice, Vairamuthan suddenly touches her hand to stop her from continuing to serve! The moment his hand touches hers, an unknown warmth spreads through her from the crown of her head to the soles of her feet. She does not snatch her hand away. She leaves it joined with his. Could Vairamuthan endure any more than that?

If even sages who had renounced everything, when confronted by a maiden fashioned by gathering together not only all the beauty found on earth but also the beauty of the heavens, could become Viswamitras at the sight of Menaka, could Vairamuthan’s youth-filled feelings alone remain crippled

<!-- source boundary: scan 63 → scan 64 -->

and inert? Realizing that she has no wish to withdraw her hand from the hand that stopped her, he gives that flower-soft hand a kiss—“ich!”

“Hey! You arrogant fellow! How much insolence have you got?” Karutha Adappan pulls the dagger tucked at his waist and springs at Vairamuthan!

“Anna, stop! Don’t touch him!” Kalyani warns Adappan and steps between them.

“Fool! First learn to keep your sister under control!” Vairamuthan lifts a cudgel and raises it against him. Vaalukku Veli, Vallatharayan, Vellai Ayyar and the other guests rush up from their places. Kaadai and Kolathari spill all the feast dishes onto the floor and run. Meganathan stands there pale and shaken.

“Kalyani! Is this the humiliation you bring upon a brother whose love for you is vaster than the sea? A disgrace never seen in our lineage! How could you dare sow the seed of a shame unknown in any of the nadus of our people—lands famed as fertile fields of culture? Chi! Having shaken the honour of the clan, you must no longer remain alive! Either you must die, or I must die! I do not have the courage to cut down with my own hand the banner of our lineage that I myself raised. So I shall take the punishment for your fault upon myself.”

<!-- source boundary: scan 64 → scan 65 -->

Vaalukku Veli raises the dagger and drives it sharply into his own chest.

“Anna! Anna!” Kalyani screams and faints!

Only when she regains consciousness and opens her eyes does Kalyani realize that Vaalukku Veli is standing beside her without any wound—and that everything that had happened before was only the mischievous play of her own uncontrollable love-imagination for Vairamuthan!

Her shock does not last long! Afraid that the feast might be disrupted because of her, she immediately gets up and asks her brother Vaalukku Veli to return to the dining hall. Then, seeing the Pattamangalam people standing around where she is, she lowers her head in embarrassment.

Seeing Vairamuthan among them, his face drawn with concern over her collapse, gives her great comfort. Vallatharayan asks Vaalukku Veli a question that feels like an axe splitting the centre of his forehead:

“Does your sister often suffer seizures like this?”

Vellai Ayyar at once rejects that inauspicious question!

“I have known Kalyani from the time she was a tiny little girl. She has no illness of that sort at all. The child suddenly became overcome with emotion somehow. That is why she fainted. That is all.”

<!-- source boundary: scan 65 → scan 66 -->

Though Vellai Ayyar’s answer is sufficient for Vallatharayan, Vaalukku Veli’s heart swings without peace: Pattamangalam’s Ambalakkarar had actually asked, “Seizures?” The thought wounds him. His hardened heart, capable of handling so many troubles, bears this too.

Everyone returns to the dining hall and sits down again. Vairamuthan too sits beside Vallatharayan. Kaadai and Kolathari run about serving. Vellai Ayyar happily swallows four or five whole eggs.

Now fully recovered, Kalyani stands by the lattice window of her room and keeps watching Vairamuthan eat in the dining hall! Fearful that those old clouds of thought might once again gather and strike her with thunder, lightning, storm and flood, she restrains herself and loses herself instead in the sight of that handsome man eating.

“How is the food? It is all the handiwork of my sister Kalyani! Why, it was from the exhaustion of cooking that Kalyani even fainted!”

Pattamangalam’s critique comes at once in answer to Paganeri’s affectionate question. Vallatharayan begins describing the food...

<!-- source boundary: scan 66 → scan 67 -->

“Excellent rabbit! A very young fawn! Chicken! Fine young birds!”

Vaalukku Veli swells with pride! Kalyani’s heart cools with relief! Vallatharayan continues!

“But look at one thing! At the speed the rabbit and fawn ran, and at the speed the chicken and the other birds flew, none of the salt, tamarind or chilli seems to have stuck to their bodies!”

Vaalukku Veli’s face tightens! Kalyani’s face darkens! Karutha Adappan replies with a laugh!

“You hunt rabbits and deer after rubbing them with salt, tamarind and chilli! We are not like that! We hunt them alive! That is why there is no salt, tamarind or chilli on the bodies of these rabbits and deer!”

Without showing his laughter, Vaalukku Veli praises his younger brother’s quickness in his heart. Kalyani has only one fear—that some quarrel may grow out of this!

“My elder brother is teasing deliberately, only for fun. I too have eaten at so many feasts! People speak of deva-amirtham, the nectar of the gods; I have never seen it! I am seeing it here now. I am eating it. Nala’s cooking cannot stand before this! This might even be called Damayanti’s cooking!”

After Vairamuthan’s words of praise, it was as though a rain of ice fell upon the crown of Kalyani’s head.

<!-- source boundary: scan 67 → scan 68 -->

“Damayanti? No, no; I do not want that name for my sister! Wasn’t Damayanti the woman whose husband abandoned her in the forest in pitch darkness, leaving her to suffer in a wilderness without even knowing the direction in which he had gone?”

At that moment, more than her brother’s teasing, it was Vairamuthan’s praise that gave Kalyani sweetness mixed from sugar-candy and honey.

The feast ends; now comes the scene of taking leave! Kalyani too comes and stands near her brother. Vallatharayan embraces Vaalukkuveli and takes leave. Vairamuthan and Karutha Adappan embrace one another.

At that moment Kalyani, half hidden behind a pillar near her brother, unconsciously embraces and strokes the pillar with her hands. Kalyani—Vairamuthan; four eyes meet! It was because Vallatharayan had noticed this from the very beginning, both at the temple and at the place where they held the chariot rope, that he had asked, “Does Kalyani suffer seizures?” His calculation was that perhaps this would cause some change in Vairamuthan’s mind!

The warriors exchange wishes and take leave of one another! They bid farewell! Four eyes separate though they cannot bear to part!

Before Kalyani’s eyes there is an emptiness like a dense forest in which a storm has knocked down every tree! At the same time there is an unshakeable confidence that they will somehow meet again.

<!-- source boundary: scan 68 → scan 69 -->

Only Vellai Ayyar remains after the feast, still talking with Vaalukku Veli.

Meanwhile the mind of the Paganeri Ambalakkarar is sunk entirely in trying to discover the cause of Kalyani’s sudden shock. So many Ambalakkarars have come to their house for feasts. On all those occasions Kalyani never even went near the kitchen—why was she so eager this time? Without even noticing that I am watching from the corner of my eye, Vairamuthan and Kalyani were looking at one another with their eyes... Chi! Chi! That must never happen!

“Ayyar, have you made any inquiries about Kalyani’s marriage? Is there some suitable bridegroom...?”

“How could I not have? Sons-in-law from so many Ambalakkarar households are competing, each saying, ‘Me first! Me first!’”

“I have made a decision. I will not give Kalyani in marriage to a rural place. Only if I find a bridegroom of standing from a good, civilized town-side family... will my heart be satisfied!”

“What is difficult about that! In the towns there are young men from good royal families and zamindar families who have received higher education. But there is one thing: will those young men marry village girls?”

“Is that so? From what you say, it looks as though my wish will not be fulfilled!”

<!-- source boundary: scan 69 → scan 70 -->

“The young men in the towns ask: Does the girl know the veena? Can she sing? Can she dance?”

“Kalyani learned dance when she was a small child! By now she must have forgotten it completely!”

“So what? If a skilled dance teacher teaches her, Kalyani can very easily become a great dancer!”

“Do you know any dance teachers like that, Ayyar?”

“There are plenty! If you call them, they will gladly teach her!”

“Tell me who! Kalyani shall become accomplished in Bharatanatyam, singing and veena! Let her become the daughter-in-law of a royal lineage or a great zamindar household in the town-side! That is my prayer!”
