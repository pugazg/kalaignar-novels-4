# 2

Like the cupped palms of a maiden brought close together, with only the fingers spreading and stirring, rows upon rows of *paalis* in which nine kinds of grain had sprouted were set out in the homes of the two nadus, Paganeri and Pattamangalam. Women with beautiful eyes in which black beetles seemed to roll celebrated the *mulaippari* festival joyfully, the sound of the bangles stacked upon their hands turning into music.

In Paganeri, people held tiny, lovely *paalis* in their hands and sang blessings for Vaalukku Veli! In Pattamangalam, they sang blessings for their leader Vallatharayan! Those people were skilled enough to judge the fertility of the soil and foresee what the year's harvest across their fields and uplands would be like by seeing how quickly and luxuriantly the *mulaippari* sprouted and swayed in the *paali*.

Like a “puthavanam” firework placed on the ground and lit, the *mulaippari* had grown upward, richly flourishing toward the sky! At house-fronts and temple entrances, the dancing, singing, and celebrations of the *mulaippari* festival came to an end, and the *mulaippari* were set afloat in bodies of water.

<!-- source boundary: scan 17 → scan 18 -->

When even the Pillaiyar images that receive offerings of *kozhukattai*, *modakam*, ritual food, and worship on Pillaiyar Chaturthi are thrown into a river or pond, would people refrain from putting into the water the *mulaippari* that were sown yesterday, sprouted today, and would rot tomorrow?

At Pattamangalam, the Pillaiyar temple of the village occupied the central place in the *mulaippari* festival! Men, women, and children circled the temple again and again, singing a Ganapati protective song and praying as well for their Ambalakkarar, Vallatharayan.

Then the drum sounded, announcing that Vallatharayan was coming to the temple for worship. Everyone gathered at the shrine turned respectfully toward the direction from which the sound came and watched intently. Bowing, they moved aside so that they would not stand across Vallatharayan's path.

A moustache like a black sickle—the majestic stride of a tiger—one hand resting on the sword hanging at his waist—a round, flat turban studded with pearls—that heroic figure was Vallatharayan! A pearl necklace set with a tiger claw rolled upon his chest.

Beside him was an attractive young man! A firmly built body! He walked like a leopard beside a tiger! He was Vallatharayan's younger brother, the young prince Vairamuthan! Since no turban concealed it,

one could see the beauty of his curling hair, which had grown somewhat long. It was not only the two brothers; their sister Veerammal too, along with her husband Urangaap Puli—whose figure was like that of a mighty elephant—came with them.

The people gathered there considered it a great blessing that the Ambalakkarar family, who stood like a fortress for their Pattamangalam Nadu, had come to worship at the Pillaiyar temple and lend distinction to the *mulaippari* festival.

Nodding his head in blessing to those who bowed, Ambalakkarar Vallatharayan entered the shrine with his family! The priest welcomed Vallatharayan and the others with a smile, chanted mantras while showing incense and flame to Vinayakar, then, according to custom, bestowed the honour of the silk *parivattam* and gave them *prasadam*.

“Brother! Have you noticed that this year there is a glow on our Vinayakar's face?”

Hearing the voice of his sister Veerammal, Vallatharayan turned eagerly toward her.

“Yes! It's true! There is a glow! The reason is that the priest has lit too much camphor.”

That was the explanation offered by Veerammal's husband—Vallatharayan's brother-in-law, Urangaap Puli!

Hearing it, Vallatharayan laughed and asked Veerammal, “What reason are you going to give, dear?

<!-- source boundary: scan 19 → scan 20 -->

Do you reject the reason your husband Urangaappuli gives, or do you accept it?”

“Everything is a joke to him! There is a proper reason why, unlike any other year, our Pattamangalam Vinayakar shines with such radiance this year! My brother is going to be married this year.” A special brightness came over Veerammal's face.

“Veeramma! How many times must I tell you? ‘If I marry and an heir is born to me, won't my dear younger brother Vairamuthan here lose his chance to assume the office of Ambalakkarar of Pattamangalam? That is why I have taken a vow never to marry. So do not start that talk again!’”

Moved by Vallatharayan's brotherly affection, Vairamuthan trembled with emotion as though to protest his elder brother's resolve and broke in with, “Brother!”

“Younger brother! It is a settled decision! Even this Vinayakar cannot change me in this matter.”

Overwhelmed by the flood of his brother's affection, his eyes filling like pools, Vairamuthan touched Vallatharayan's feet and bowed. As they dispersed, the villagers spoke softly among themselves: this is how brothers ought to be.

<!-- source boundary: scan 20 → scan 21 -->

“What? Paganeri and Pattamangalam's *mulaippari* festival? What a grand commotion!”

A figure approached with a broad smile and came to stand beside the Ambalakkarar family near the sanctum.

“Oh! Vellai Ayyar! Welcome, welcome! Where are you coming from?” Vallatharayan greeted him warmly.

“Where else would I come from? Madurai, of course! Don't you know what happened? Paganeri Vaalukku Veli's younger brother, Karutha Adappan, won the wrestling contest at the Madurai Nayak king's palace, didn't he? This very morning he was presented with a ‘valour award’ at the Nayak king's court! Vaalukku Veli insisted that I come and bless his younger brother, so I went to Madurai and am just returning.”

Instead of making lines of happiness flash across Vallatharayan's face, the news of victory and the award ceremony brought by Vellai Ayyar made his face look like a sky gathering rain clouds. Yet he tried to master himself and said, “Oh! Is that so! Splendid! Splendid!”—trying, unsuccessfully, to summon happiness.

Throughout the southern Pandya country everyone called Vellai Ayyar “Madurai Vellai Ayyar.” He followed the white men's mode of dress by wearing coat and suit, yet, as proof that he had not abandoned old custom either, he wore a turban as well,

<!-- source boundary: scan 21 → scan 22 -->

smeared sacred ash thickly across his forehead, and presented himself as a gentleman of substance!

At a time when the dominance of the white rulers was gradually spreading, when those who opposed them were turned into Kattabommans and those who supported them became Ettappans who gained thrones, and when Tamilakam was sinking into the dust of subjugation, Madurai Venkataramana Ayyar looked ahead politically, read the direction of the future, adopted the pseudonym Vellai Ayyar, mastered the art of crossing the flood of time, put that art into practice, and began to succeed.

To support the ascendancy of the white rulers, he took the word “Vellai”; in the name of preserving old customs, he joined to it the word “Ayyar”; thus he became Vellai Ayyar! But he was no venomous creature!

The news that Vaalukku Veli's younger brother Karutha Adappan had planted the flag of victory in the wrestling contest conducted by the Madurai Nayak king and received an award made Vallatharayan angry even with Vellai Ayyar.

Somehow, without even fully knowing why, he could not rid himself of the hostility that had arisen in him toward Paganeri Ambalakkarar Vaalukku Veli and Karutha Adappan. He could win the affection of the people of Pattamangalam. He could stand as a symbol of brotherly love. Yet some kind of resentment toward Vaalukku Veli and Karutha Adappan had taken root in him.

<!-- source boundary: scan 22 → scan 23 -->

Do they not say that, sometimes, even without any reason, the fire of jealousy scorches the heart? Perhaps that was Vallatharayan's condition! Or perhaps an inherited feud between the Paganeri Ambalakkarar family and the Pattamangalam Ambalakkarar family, long lying as a sprout, had now put out branches—who could say?

Realising that the news brought by Vellai Ayyar had somehow taken the shine off the *mulaippari* festival at Pattamangalam, Vairamuthan, Veerammal, Urangaappuli, and the others maintained silence while watching Vallatharayan's face.

Then, breaking that silence, came the sound of a horse's hooves approaching at speed. Every person turned toward the sound. Karutha Adappan, younger brother of Vaalukku Veli, pulled the reins of the fast-approaching horse and brought it to a halt. He dismounted at the entrance of the Pattamangalam Pillaiyar temple, patted the horse, and walked toward Vallatharayan's family.
