# 9

In the end, the dance teacher Vellai Ayyar had mentioned lived in nearby Tirukkottiyur. The town was one of the sacred places of Tirumal. During the temple chariot festivals there, the Pattamangalam Ambalakkarar would customarily receive ceremonial honours from the temple.

Beautiful streets around the temple, a tank facing it, a flower garden where blossoms needed for worship were grown, and a magnificent gopuram with a golden covering gave Tirukkottiyur a charm of its own! The chariot of that town was an offering of the Maruthu Pandiyars.

There also stood a stone-and-mortar building that, according to the narration, Sivagangai’s queen Velu Nachiyar—who fought a valiant war against the white men—had built for wartime needs.

That small town had an importance in the pages of history and in puranic and epic accounts! It is also said that Ramanujar leapt from the gopuram of that town!

As though adding still more distinction to a place of such distinction, Sundarambal, who shone as an empress of dance,

<!-- source boundary: scan 71 → scan 72 -->

also lived in that very town. A little away from the street stood two houses facing each other. Sundarambal lived in one! Her beloved younger sister Vadivambal lived in the other! She too was renowned in dance!

Sundarambal may have been about twenty years old; Vadivambal was two or three years younger. Only the houses stood opposite each other—for three quarters of every day Vadivambal would be at her elder sister Sundarambal’s house! No one in that area thought less of them merely because they were queens of the art of dance.

There are, are there not, hasty people who, the moment they hear that women belong to the arts, regard them as commodities for sale? Whatever the field—even if women live under strict discipline in domestic life, even within the prison of the home—who can restrain a woman whose mind is determined to stray? The circumstances particular to the arts can make the women who enter them susceptible more easily. That is all!

The Kural,

“What use is guarding women by confinement?
The guard of their own virtue is the foremost guard,”

explains: “Women cannot have their character protected merely by being imprisoned; only the quality of chastity, the innate completeness that belongs to them, can truly protect them!”

Sundarambal had made it her aim to live according to this Kural herself and to have her younger sister Vadivambal live the same way!

<!-- source boundary: scan 72 → scan 73 -->

That was why she could live there on a footing equal to some of the mitta-miras landholders scattered through the area! She was an ivory statue! Her younger sister was a golden statue! At least four or five dance performances a month would keep taking place in many towns, sometimes as temple-festival events and sometimes as wedding concerts. In the accompanying ensemble for the performances, Nathamuni on nattuvangam and the supporting singer Lalithangi were important. Most of the time they would alternate between Sundari’s house and Vadivu’s house, gossiping, eating and sleeping there. In a way, they were almost relatives to Sundarambal!

That day Sundarambal was very tired because she had spent a long time rehearsing a new dance performance. Though she normally went to the temple, that day she sent Vadivu and Lalithangi and remained alone at home. Nattuvangam Nathamuni, meanwhile, was lying on the veranda of Vadivu’s house, already snoring in the evening.

Sundari bolted the street door and began removing her clothes so that she could bathe. The full-length mirror in the hall reflected the beauty of her half-clad form.

“Hot water is ready, Amma!” called the maid. Sundari tied around herself a thin cloth that gave some cover above the breasts and below the girdle ornaments, then entered the bathroom. The hot water was washing away all the weariness of dancing.

<!-- source boundary: scan 73 → scan 74 -->

In the bathroom that little cloth hung abandoned on a wire clothesline. Once the bath was over, good fortune struck it! For a little while it clung to her rain-cloud-dark hair! Then it danced upon Sundari’s moon-like face! Next came that conch-like neck! And after that, it caressed and played over parts of that marble body no one had ever seen! Sundari opened the bathroom door, stretched only her hand outside and called the maid; taking the sari the maid gave her, she wrapped herself in it and came out. Poor cloth—it lay pitifully on the floor! It had one consolation: the same opportunity might come again in the next day’s bath!

After standing before the mirror and adorning herself, Sundarambal told the maid to hurry the cooking, then sat on the swing in the hall and rocked gently.

Hearing a knock at the street door, she herself rose and opened it. Two men stood at the entrance ingratiatingly. Their faces made it clear that they belonged to that region.

“We came to arrange a performance—may we come in?”

“Please do!”

The two looked at each other and exchanged some meaning through their expressions as they entered. Sundari pointed to two chairs in the hall and asked them to sit.

<!-- source boundary: scan 74 → scan 75 -->

“What performance? In which town?”

“Colonel Durai has set up camp at Kattambur in order to capture the Maruthu Pandiyars.”

“Do those Parangi sinners have no other work? Will they attend to anything else only after destroying every warrior born on this soil?”

“What can we do? Whether they are good people or bad, their power keeps growing! We too may as well join the town, shout ‘Jey!’ and get on with it!”

“How are we ever going to prosper while there are people who change colour to suit the moment!”

“Leave that aside; why should we involve ourselves in all that? The main reason we came was...”

“Tell me!”

“Your performance is urgently and necessarily needed today.”

“None of the accompanists are here! How can you tell me all of a sudden?”

“Does Colonel Durai lack accompanists? They are all genuine foreign stock!”

“What are you saying?”

“Don’t you understand? Tonight you have to come to the place where Durai has set up camp! You have to make him happy! For that, whatever amount you ask...”

<!-- source boundary: scan 75 → scan 76 -->

“Chi! Get up! Do you think that because this is a house of the Muse, it is the house of a woman for sale? Leave respectfully!”

“Sundari! Don’t talk so sharply! In a little while these white durais are going to rule this entire region. If you don’t submit to them...?”

“What if I do not submit? My life will go! That is all, isn’t it?”

“First your honour will go—only then will your life go! Don’t be stubborn for nothing! It is better if you come with us! Otherwise Colonel Durai’s sepoys will come and drag you away!”

“I will see even that. First you leave.”

“Why do we need sepoys? We ourselves will take you along right now.”

The two men close in around Sundarambal.

“Ayyo, Amma!” the maid screams as she runs in. Before she can arrive, one of the men catches Sundarambal’s hand and begins pulling her.

“Chi! Let go of my hand!” Sundari snaps, wrenching herself away. At that moment a warrior leaps inside, gripping both men tightly by the backs of their necks.

<!-- source boundary: scan 76 → scan 77 -->

“Chi! Are you Tamils? Have you started doing even this sort of work for that white durai fellow? Thu! Have you no shame?”

The forceful question sounds like a lion’s roar!

“Hey—who are you? Let go of our necks! If what you’ve done reaches Colonel Durai’s ears, a bullet will tear through the hollow of your chest. Be careful!”

“You are threatening me?”

After this battle of words, the warrior knocks the two men’s heads against each other and enjoys the sight! Enraged by what he has done, the two spring at him! For a short while there is a fight in the hall of Sundari’s house! Colonel Durai’s thugs are knocked about like balls! When they try to run because they cannot bear the blows, the warrior seizes them firmly. Astonished by his strength, Sundari stares at him without blinking. The beaten men do not fail to keep up their verbal bravado!

“Hey—once we know who you are, that is enough! Colonel Durai’s pistol will play upon you.”

“Do you want to know who I am? Tell your Colonel Durai that I am Karutha Adappan, younger brother of Paganeri Ambalakkarar Vaalukku Veli Thevar!”

At the sound of that name, Colonel Durai’s men tremble.

“Karutha Adappan? Ayyo! Forgive us! We came without knowing that this was a place connected with you!

Even if Durai himself knew, he would be angry with us. We are leaving!” Saying this, the two men take to their heels.

Sundari looks at Adappan with tear-filled eyes. He looks back at her with sympathy.

“Thank you very much!”

“It is all right! I’ll be going!”...

“At least have a bowl of milk in our house for the help you gave us in danger.”

No! Thank you very much... I...” Karutha Adappan opens his mouth as though to say something, then stops.

“Somehow you came at just the right time!”

“I came here to see you in the first place!”

Sundarambal’s face grows still more unsettled. Even so, she speaks with him.

“To see me? Why?”

“My elder brother sent me to speak about my younger sister!”

“About your younger sister? Then...?”

“You do not know all the details about our family!”

“Even if I do not, I have heard about the Paganeri Ambalakkarar and about you, his younger brother...” —
